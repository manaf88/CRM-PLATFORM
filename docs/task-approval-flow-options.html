الخلاصة بالعربي

هذا المستند فيه أربعة مسارات لحركة الشغل: مين ينفّذ، مين يوافق، وشو يصير لما ترجع ملاحظات. كل مسار مكتوب فيه شو بده تطوير وشو جاهز اليوم، وفي جدول مقارنة بالآخر.

توصيتي بصفحة التوصية: المسار رقم ٣ للجزء الداخلي مع المسار رقم ٤ للجزء اللي يشوفه العميل — والسبب إنه نص الشغل مبني أصلاً بالمنصة وما حدا منتبه إله.

1The flow you described

Taking the example exactly as it was given — an account manager hands out a task, the copy is written, it comes back for approval, then it goes to design, and the design lead approves before anything leaves the agency:

Account ManagerCreates the task, assigns itto self or to someone else
→
Jessika · CopywriterWrites the plan / the copydoing the work
→
Account ManagerApproves or sends backgate 1
→
Omar · Design leadPasses design to Yasinhand-off
→
Yasin · DesignerProduces the designdoing the work
→
Omar · Design leadApproves or sends backgate 2

↩ Sent back at either gate → returns to the person who did the work, with a note. Nobody outside the agency sees it until both gates pass.

المسار كما وصفته

مدير الحساب يفتح المهمة ويكلّف حاله أو غيره. جيسيكا تكتب الخطة، ترجع لمدير الحساب يوافق أو يرفض. بعدها تنتقل لعمر — وهو مثل المسؤول عن التصميم — فيمرّرها لياسين، وياسين يصمّم، وترجع لعمر يوافق أو يطلب تعديل. ما بيطلع شي للعميل قبل ما تعدّي البوابتين.

Two things in that sentence are worth separating, because they are different problems:

  • The chain — work moving from person to person in a known order.
  • The gate — somebody with authority saying yes or no before it moves on.

The platform today handles the chain informally and has no internal gate on tasks at all. That is the whole gap.

2What the platform already has

Before choosing, it matters what is already built — one of these is a genuine surprise.

Building blocks in the system today
BlockWhat it doesFit for this flow
Tasks One assignee, a status (TODO, IN_PROGRESS, IN_REVIEW, BLOCKED, DONE), a type, a due date, comments and a full activity log Partly — carries the work, but has no approver. IN_REVIEW does not say who must review
Post approvals A real state machine: draft → internal review → ready for client → changes requested → approved → scheduled → published, with a signed log of every transition Yes — but only one internal gate, and only for content
Responsibility matrix Per client, per area of work: who is TO_WORK_ON, who is TO_APPROVE, who is TO_SUPERVISE, and seven more relationships Yes — this already models "Omar approves design on this client"
Multiple roles per person Somebody can be Designer and Copywriter on the same client (shipped this week) Yes — one person can do and approve different things
Automations Trigger → action rules, e.g. changes requested on a post creates fix-up tasks Partly — the engine exists, only two rules are wired

The surprise worth reading twice

The responsibility matrix already stores, per client and per area of work, who has TO_APPROVE and who has TO_WORK_ON. That is precisely "Omar is the boss of the designers on Taxero" — already modelled, already has a screen, and completely unused by tasks.

This is the difference between typing Omar's name onto forty tasks and setting it once for the client. When Omar is on holiday you change one cell instead of chasing every open task.

نقطة مهمة

في المنصة شي اسمه مصفوفة المسؤوليات، وفيها لكل عميل ولكل مجال شغل: مين TO_WORK_ON ومين TO_APPROVE. يعني فكرة «عمر هو المسؤول عن التصميم عند هذا العميل» موجودة ومبنية — بس المهام ما بتستعملها. أهم توصية بهذا الملف إننا نربطهم ببعض، حتى ما نكتب اسم عمر يدوياً بكل مهمة، ولما يسافر نغيّر خانة وحدة بدل ما نلاحق كل المهام المفتوحة.

3The four options

1 Pass the task around works today · no build

تمرير المهمة نفسها بين الأشخاص

One task per deliverable. The chain is expressed by changing who it is assigned to and moving the status. Approval is a conversation in the comments.

JessikaWrites, then reassigns to the AMIN_PROGRESS → IN_REVIEW
→
Account ManagerReads it, reassigns to OmarIN_REVIEW → TODO
→
Omar → YasinDesign, then back to OmarIN_PROGRESS → IN_REVIEW
→
OmarMarks it doneDONE

↩ Rejection = a comment plus setting the status back and reassigning by hand.

Good: you can start on Monday, with zero development. For a small team that talks all day, it is often enough.

Bad: nothing records that Omar approved — only that the status changed. The task's history shows a chain of reassignments, so "who wrote this" is lost once it moves on. The dashboard cannot tell work-in-progress from work-waiting-for-a-boss, so "we are blocked on approvals" never shows up as a number.

مهمة وحدة تنتقل بتغيير المسؤول والحالة. يشتغل من اليوم بدون تطوير، بس ما في إثبات إن عمر وافق فعلاً، وبتضيع معرفة مين كتب الأصل، والداشبورد ما بيقدر يقول لك «الشغل واقف على موافقات».

2 A separate task for every stage small build

مهمة مستقلة لكل مرحلة

One parent deliverable, and a child task per stage. Each has its own assignee and due date. Finishing one opens the next automatically.

JessikaWrite copytask 1
→
Account ManagerReview copytask 2
→
YasinDesigntask 3
→
OmarReview designtask 4
→
Social mediaPublishtask 5

↩ Rejection reopens the previous stage's task and the reviewer's task waits.

Needs: a parent/child link on tasks, a rule that opens the next stage, and a template so the account manager clicks once instead of creating five tasks.

Good: every person's list contains only their own step. You can measure how long each stage really takes — "design review sits for two days" becomes a fact instead of a feeling.

Bad: five rows for one post. Without a template it is more admin work than it saves, and the task list gets long fast.

مهمة لكل مرحلة: كتابة، مراجعة، تصميم، مراجعة تصميم، نشر. كل واحد يشوف مهمته فقط، وبتقدر تقيس كم بتاخد كل مرحلة. العيب: خمس مهام لكل منشور، ولازم قالب جاهز وإلا بيصير شغل إداري أكتر من فايدته.

3 One task, with a named approver small build recommended half

مهمة واحدة مع «مُعتمِد»

The task keeps one row, but gains a second person: the approver. The doer presses Submit for review; it lands in the approver's queue. Approve moves it to the next stage; request changes sends it back with a note and the doer keeps it.

Jessika · doerSubmits for reviewIN_PROGRESS → IN_REVIEW
→
AM · approverApprove / request changesapproval queue
→
Yasin · doerDesigns, submitsstage advances
→
Omar · approverApprove / request changesapproval queue
→
—Ready to publishDONE

↩ Request changes → back to the same doer, note attached, approval clock stops. The row never changes hands, so history stays intact.

Needs: an approver on the task, three actions (submit, approve, request changes), and notifications. The approver is looked up from the responsibility matrix instead of typed — Omar approves design on Taxero because the matrix says so.

Good: this is the flow you described, exactly. One row per deliverable. "Approved by Omar at 14:20" becomes a real record. "Waiting for approval" becomes a number the dashboard can show and age, the same way client approvals already age today.

Bad: it needs building — roughly three to four days of backend, plus the screens.

مهمة وحدة بس معها «مُعتمِد». المنفّذ يضغط «أرسل للمراجعة»، فتظهر عند المُعتمِد بقائمة الموافقات. يوافق فتنتقل للمرحلة اللي بعدها، أو يطلب تعديل فترجع لنفس المنفّذ مع ملاحظة. المُعتمِد بيتحدّد تلقائياً من مصفوفة المسؤوليات مش بالكتابة اليدوية. هذا أقرب مسار لاللي وصفته، وبصير عنا رقم حقيقي اسمه «شغل واقف على موافقة».

4 Content rides the post, not the task works today recommended half

المحتوى يمشي على المنشور نفسه

For anything that ends as a published post, the post itself is the deliverable and already has a full approval machine. Tasks only tell people to go and do the work.

Copy + designBuilt on the postDRAFT
→
InternalAgency reviewIN_INTERNAL_REVIEW
→
ClientApproves or asks for changesREADY_FOR_CLIENT
→
Social mediaSchedule and publishSCHEDULED → PUBLISHED

↩ The client asking for changes already creates the fix-up tasks automatically, and the waiting time is already measured on the dashboard.

Needs: nothing. Every arrow above is built, logged and already counted in the Super Admin dashboard.

Good: the half of the process that touches the client — the risky, slow, "who is sitting on this" half — is done and working.

Bad: only one internal gate exists. Omar-approves-Yasin and the AM-approves-Jessika cannot both be internal gates on the post. And it covers content only — reports, follow-ups and ad hoc work still need tasks.

المنشور نفسه عنده حالات جاهزة: مسودة ← مراجعة داخلية ← مراجعة العميل ← مجدول ← منشور، وكلها مبنية وشغالة اليوم مع احتساب مدة انتظار العميل. الناقص: بوابة داخلية وحدة بس، يعني ما بتكفّي لموافقة مدير الحساب وموافقة عمر مع بعض — وهون بيجي المسار رقم ٣.

5Recommendation

Option 3 for inside the agency + Option 4 for the client

They are not competitors — they cover different halves. Option 4 is already built and handles everything from "the agency is happy" to "it is published, and here is how long the client sat on it". Option 3 adds the missing half: the internal chain with real gates, which is exactly what was described.

Concretely, one post would run like this:

JessikaCopy task, submitsoption 3
→
AMApprovesgate 1
→
YasinDesign task, submitsoption 3
→
OmarApprovesgate 2
→
ClientApprovesoption 4 · built
→
Social mediaPublishesoption 4 · built

Why not the other two

  • Option 1 is the honest "start Monday" answer, and it is a reasonable stopgap while Option 3 is built. It is not a destination: the moment somebody asks "why was this late", nothing in it can answer.
  • Option 2 is not wrong — it is Option 3 with more rows. Choose it instead only if you want each stage to carry its own separate due date and separate owner in reporting. If you only want approval gates, Option 3 gives you the same control with a fifth of the clutter.

التوصية بالعربي

ناخد المسار ٣ للجزء الداخلي (جيسيكا ← مدير الحساب ← ياسين ← عمر) والمسار ٤ للجزء اللي بيشوفه العميل، لأنه جاهز وشغّال من اليوم. مش خيارين متنافسين — كل واحد بيغطي نص.

ومين يوافق ياخده النظام من مصفوفة المسؤوليات (TO_APPROVE) بدل ما نكتب اسم عمر بكل مهمة.

لحد ما يخلص التطوير، اشتغلوا بالمسار ١ — بس مش حل دائم، لأنه أول ما تسأل «ليش تأخّر هالشغل» ما رح تلاقي جواب مسجّل.

6Five things to decide before building

These are product rules, not technical ones. Each changes what gets built.

1 · When Omar sends the design back, who gets it?

Only Yasin, or does the whole thing restart from Jessika? Sending it back one step is the usual answer; restarting punishes people for someone else's change.

لما عمر يرجّع التصميم — بترجع لياسين بس، ولا بتبلّش من أول وجديد من عند جيسيكا؟

2 · Can the account manager skip a gate when it is urgent?

If yes, the skip should be recorded — an override that leaves a trace is fine, one that leaves none is a hole.

هل مدير الحساب يقدر يتخطّى بوابة موافقة وقت الاستعجال؟ إذا آه، لازم يتسجّل إنه تخطّاها.

3 · Is the approver a person or a role?

I recommend a role via the responsibility matrix — "whoever approves design for Taxero" — so a holiday is one edit instead of forty. There is no "design lead" role in the system today, so this may mean adding one.

المُعتمِد شخص محدّد ولا دور؟ الأفضل دور من مصفوفة المسؤوليات، حتى لما يسافر عمر نغيّر خانة وحدة. ملاحظة: ما في دور «مسؤول تصميم» بالنظام حالياً — ممكن نحتاج نضيفه.

4 · Does copy need its own gate, or only design?

Your example has two gates. Two is fine; four gates on a single Instagram post will feel like paperwork and people will route around it.

هل الكتابة بدها بوابة موافقة كمان، ولا التصميم بس؟ بوابتين منيح — أربعة على منشور واحد بتصير روتين والناس بتلتف عليها.

5 · Who presses publish?

Nothing publishes to social networks automatically today — a person still does it, and the dashboard already flags posts whose scheduled time has passed. Decide whether that person is the social media manager or the account manager.

مين يضغط «نشر»؟ النظام ما بينشر لحاله على السوشال — لسه بدها شخص. والداشبورد عم يبيّن المنشورات اللي فات وقتها. قرّروا: مسؤول السوشال ولا مدير الحساب؟

One caution from experience

Every gate you add is a place where work stops when somebody is busy. Two gates with a visible ageing clock beat four gates that people learn to bypass. Start with the two that were described, and add more only when a real mistake makes the case for one.
